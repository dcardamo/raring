No business in your business

Privacy, in plain words.

Raring doesn’t send your health data to us. There’s no Raring account, analytics, telemetry, advertising or remote crash-reporting service in the app.

This describes the Android app and raring.app.

Your phone connects directly to Garmin

Raring reads data from your Garmin account over encrypted HTTPS connections. It doesn’t connect to your watch, and your data doesn’t pass through a Raring server. Keep Garmin Connect syncing so that your latest watch data reaches Garmin.

The app only reads. It doesn’t change anything in your Garmin account, and your goals and plans in Raring are never sent to Garmin.

Garmin still processes your sign-in and requests to its services. Its sign-in page, including any cookies and scripts it uses, is governed by Garmin’s privacy policy, not this page. Raring is independent and not affiliated with Garmin.

Your sign-in

You sign in on Garmin’s own page inside the app, including multi-factor authentication if enabled. Raring doesn’t read or store your password. It receives sign-in tokens so it can download data and stay signed in.

On Android, these tokens are encrypted with a key held in Android Keystore and stored in the app’s no-backup storage.

What is stored on your phone

Raring stores Garmin responses in a local database, including your Garmin profile, daily health and training metrics, sleep, and activities. Workout data may include GPS routes. Sync prioritizes recent daily data, then downloads available older history in the background.

This database is in Android’s private app storage. Raring does not add a separate database encryption layer; it relies on Android’s app isolation and device encryption. The app disables Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer for its data.

Your goals, planned sessions, rest days, pauses, limits, routines, events, saved sleep plans, notification preferences and sync state are also local. Raring has no server-side copy of any of it.

Garmin journal reads are off until you allow them. Turning them off stops new reads and hides the journal, but previously downloaded logs stay on your device and remain in exports until local data is erased. Journal entries are never included in notifications.

Settings → Export creates a ZIP of saved Garmin responses, daily CSVs and Raring settings, never sign-in tokens. The export leaves your phone only when you choose to share it. Raring removes its temporary share file from the app’s cache about a day after the export is made; a copy you save or share is under your control.

Settings → Import reads an export you choose on the phone and restores your goals, plans, limits, routines and events from it. It refuses an export from a different Garmin account, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Removing your local data

Use Settings → Sign out and erase this phone’s data to remove your saved sign-in, downloaded Garmin history, journal logs, goals, plans, limits and routines from Raring. You can also clear Raring’s storage in Android settings or uninstall it. None of these actions deletes the original data in your Garmin account.

If your Garmin session expires, Raring may ask you to sign in again while keeping previously downloaded history on your phone. Android verifies the account before showing cached data. Reconnecting the same account preserves local history, goals and plans; signing into a different account clears the previous account’s cached data and local settings. Export first if you need to keep those settings.

Notifications, widgets and diagnostics

Notifications are created on your phone, not sent through a Raring push service. They may contain health information visible on your lock screen. Turn them off in Raring’s Settings, or adjust their visibility in Android.

Home-screen widgets show readings such as readiness, sleep score, Body Battery and stress to anyone who can see your home screen; remove them if that isn’t what you want. Widgets and the optional status notification never show your limits, injury notes, routines, journal entries or weight.

The app may write basic sync status and error messages to Android’s local system log. It does not upload logs or crash reports to us.

This website and downloads

This is a static GitHub Pages site. It has no analytics, tracking scripts, cookies set by Raring, forms or embedded third-party content. Fonts and images are served with the site.

Hosting is not the same as no data processing: GitHub receives your IP address and ordinary web-request information when you visit this site or download a release. GitHub may keep this information for security and operating its services. See GitHub’s privacy statement.

Following links to Garmin, GitHub or Obtainium takes you to services with their own privacy practices.

Questions or problems?

The source code is public. You can ask questions or report problems in GitHub Issues. Issues are public too: don’t post passwords, tokens, health records or screenshots containing personal information.
